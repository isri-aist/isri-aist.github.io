# Consortium for Social Implementation of Autonomous Mobility Services

> **Concept under preparation.** This page summarizes a proposal for establishing the consortium. It does not announce formal establishment, open membership applications, or confirmed governance arrangements.

## Purpose

Although Level 4 automated-driving demonstrations have advanced, sustainable deployment of autonomous mobility services still faces common challenges in passenger services, remote operation, business viability, and regulatory procedures.

The proposed consortium aims to bring together **researchers and developers, transport operators, local governments, industry, and academia** to discuss and address challenges in the pre-competitive domain from **technical, operational, and regulatory** perspectives.

Its goals are to help solve public transport challenges, promote related industries, and contribute to safe, secure, and comfortable mobility.

## Background

AIST coordinated activities under **RoAD to the L4** (FY2021–FY2025), a project involving Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry and Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism. Experiences include initiatives in Eiheiji, the Hitachi BRT, and Kashiwa-no-ha. The proposed consortium seeks to apply these experiences to shared deployment challenges beyond individual demonstrations.

## Proposed activities

| Area | Activities |
| --- | --- |
| **Exchange and networking** | Survey and share domestic and international developments |
| **Problem-solving support** | Discuss challenges and share verification data and demonstration evaluations |
| **Project support** | Develop and promote collaborative projects |
| **Outreach** | Disseminate information through symposia and the web |
| **Other activities** | Additional activities necessary to achieve the consortium's objectives |

### Examples of issues to explore

- **Standing passengers on automated buses:** balancing passenger service and safety
- **Remote supervision:** operating multiple vehicles with a small number of supervisors
- **Operations and business viability:** sustainable services and lower deployment costs
- **Regulation and authorization:** identifying shared barriers and possible solutions

These are illustrative topics, not announcements of formally established working groups.

## Proposed organization

The proposal envisages a general assembly, steering committee, secretariat, and working groups. The secretariat is planned to be hosted by the Intelligent Systems Research Institute at AIST. Appointments and working groups remain provisional.

## Expected outcomes

- Accelerated deployment of autonomous mobility services
- Reduced deployment costs through shared knowledge and data
- Progress in addressing regulatory challenges
- New joint research, demonstrations, and projects
- Stronger industry–academia–government collaboration for sustainable regional transport
